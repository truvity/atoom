# The card session and a restarted driver

What the `key/yubikeypiv` driver does to the card's session when it starts, unlocks and ends, so that a card left PIN-verified by an earlier process is never trusted.

A YubiKey stays PIN-verified for as long as it is powered and its session is not reset, whichever process verified it. A driver that ends does not log the card out by itself, so a driver that is started again meets a card that its predecessor left logged in. That state is wrong in two ways. The driver says locked while the card is open to anything that reaches `pcscd` on the machine. And the card answers the empty `VERIFY` that reads the PIN counter with success, not with the counter, so an unlock was refused with `card`, and the journal said `the PIN counter cannot be read` with `expected error code from empty pin` (reported from a host's boards on 2026-11-07, after a restart of the driver's unit without a restart of `pcscd`; not measured by the change that fixed it). A deploy did not meet it, because it restarts `pcscd`, which resets the card.

- **The driver resets the session at its start.** Before it reads anything from the card, the driver resets the card session with `SCardConnect` (exclusive) and `SCardDisconnect` with `SCARD_RESET_CARD`, a warm reset at the PC/SC layer, and reads the PIN counter to prove that the card is logged out. A card that is still verified, or cannot be reset, stops the start with `card.session: still_verified`, `reset_failed` or `held_elsewhere`, and the host's supervisor tries again. The driver does it on the way out too, when it exits cleanly (a stop or a signal it handles). A crash or a kill does not reach that code, which is why the reset at the start is the one that counts.
- **The reset is not the applet's reset.** The warm reset powers the card on again and selects the applet again, which ends the session's security state, the PIN verification. The keys, the PIN, the PUK, the management key and the retry counters are persistent data of the applet and are not touched by it, and no PIN is sent, so no attempt is burned. The driver never calls piv-go's `Reset`, which blocks the PIN and the PUK and wipes every key. The tests run the reset on a fake card only; the real card was checked on boards (below).
- **An unlock or a peer's release meets a verified card.** If the counter reads as already verified while the driver holds no PIN, `Unlock` and the PIN release reset the session and read the counter again, and send the PIN only if that read succeeds. If it still cannot be read, or the reset fails, the answer is the refusal `card`, the journal says why (`still PIN-verified after a reset`, or `another process holds the card`), and no PIN is sent. `Status` does the same reset for a card that is verified while the driver says locked.
- **Another holder of the card.** The driver connects to the card exclusively (piv-go does, for as long as it runs), so a process that holds the card, a `pcsc_scan`, a stray `gpg` smart card daemon or a second driver, makes the driver unable to open it, or to reset it. The driver fails closed with `held_elsewhere` and does not go on with a logged-in card it could not reset. Find the holder (`sudo ss -xp | grep pcscd` lists the processes connected to `pcscd`'s socket), stop it, and the driver starts at its next try.
- **Checked on boards (measured 2026-11-07).** A plain restart of the driver with `pcscd`'s process unchanged, on two boards, three times in all: the first counter reading was `locked: true` and `pinRetries: 3`, on a card that the earlier driver process had logged in; every unlock succeeded; the counter read 3 on every reading; and the journal held no `still_verified`, `reset_failed` or `held_elsewhere`. That run predates the journal line that each reset now writes, so it shows only by its result. The line was seen the same day on three boards (below).
- **How a host recovers if the driver is stuck.** First read the driver's journal: the reason is there. If the card is held or will not reset, stop the holder and restart the driver. If that does not clear it, restart the card service, which resets the card (`pcscd.socket` and `pcscd.service`), then the driver, then unlock it again. The last resort is to unplug the YubiKey and plug it in again, which powers it off. None of these changes a PIN, a key or a counter.
- **Each reset leaves a line in the journal.** The driver writes one line for every reset of the card session, with the place (`where=start`, `unlock`, `status` or `exit`), whether the card was logged in before it (`card=logged_in`, `logged_out` or `unknown` when the card could not be asked, which it does with an empty `VERIFY`, no PIN and no attempt) and whether the driver held the PIN (`driver_holds_pin`). A card that was found logged in while the driver held no PIN is a warning, `card session reset: the card was found PIN-verified and the driver held no PIN`: something else logged it in, or an earlier driver ended without a reset, and the line is the evidence. Any other reset is an information line, `card session reset`; so is the reset at exit of a card the driver itself logged in (`driver_holds_pin=true`). A reset that fails is an error line with the same place. Search the journal for `card session reset` to list them. Measured 2026-11-07 on three boards: after each deploy one line `where=start card=logged_out driver_holds_pin=false`; after a plain restart of the driver with `pcscd`'s process unchanged, `where=exit card=logged_in driver_holds_pin=true` and then one `where=start card=logged_out`, with the PIN counter reading 3 on the locked card. A peer's PIN release that meets a logged-in card is `where=unlock`. No line holds a PIN or anything made from one.
- **An unlocked driver is asked every 30 s by its host, and says so quietly.** A host cannot be told that its driver was locked again (a restart, a card that went away), so while the driver holds the PIN a host that wants PIN release can ask it for a request every 30 s, and the driver refuses with the reason `unlocked`. That is the answer of a driver that is well. It was a warning in the driver's journal every 30 s (measured 2026-11-07, three boards, and it cluttered any check that the journal is quiet); it is a debug line now. The answer to the caller is the same, and there is no card operation. Every other refusal of the call is still a warning.
- **`Status` reads the PIN counter.** It returns the node, whether the driver holds the PIN and the attempts the card has left, read with a `VERIFY` that carries no PIN and so never burns an attempt. While the driver holds the PIN, the counter cannot be read without ending the login, and `Status` returns `-1`, unknown. With one attempt left an unlock is refused (`pin_retries`), and with none the card is blocked (`pin_blocked`); the PUK procedure is the card owner's. How a host's command shows these is the host's ([slots-pin-touch.md](slots-pin-touch.md#the-locked-start-and-the-admin-socket)).
